\section{Variability and Measurement Accounting}
\label{sec:results-variability}
\label{sec:results-elapsed}

For each input/backend, relative IQR is the R7-interpolated IQR divided by
its own twenty-batch median. Across the common set its median is 1.37\% for
the list and 1.08\% for the tree; maxima are 50.26\% and 61.81\%, respectively.
The targeted-set medians are 1.18\% and 1.55\%, with maxima 9.08\% and 7.52\%.
Thus typical cells have narrow central spreads, but some common-set cells do
not. Every batch is retained, including the high-variability cells.

One common-set sample batch is below 10 ms; none in the targeted set is.
The predetermined calibration criterion was the median of three calibration batches,
not a lower bound on every later batch. The protocol therefore retained both
the observation and the fixed batch size B.

Summed sample intervals are 183.511 seconds for the common set and 21.290
seconds for the targeted set. These are accumulated timed intervals, not
campaign wall times: worker startup, GC preparation, validation, compression,
synchronization and other orchestration occur outside them.

\section{What the Comparison Shows}
\label{sec:results-boundary}

On the predetermined inputs, the measured tree configuration has higher per-input
median runtimes despite its stronger source-level amortized bound. Removing
instrumentation hooks substantially lowers runtime in the supplementary diagnostic, although
most diagnostic inputs still favor the list. These observations make the cost
of the concrete Python implementation central to the comparison: field access,
wrappers, local checks, allocation and tree maintenance all contribute to the
measured calls. Their effects on broader workloads remain a question for
further experiments.
