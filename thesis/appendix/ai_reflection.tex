AI tools supported my work on an executable reconstruction of Jordan sorting.
I required the sorting procedure to recover its output from its own maintained
state, and I distinguished correctness, complexity arguments and measured runtime
when assessing revisions. These requirements shaped how I wanted the
reconstruction and its results to be evaluated. The choice of a red--black finger
tree as the efficient backend and the implementation of the sorting algorithm
were my own decisions and work.

During revision, I questioned whether the common set's lack of two-sided splits
left a gap in experimental coverage. The resulting discussion distinguished
twelve targeted inputs with formal timings from eighteen structural inputs that
had at that stage undergone correctness checks without formal timing. This clarified an
important limitation: variation in nesting depth does not by itself establish
coverage of the operations that distinguish the backends. It also helped me
formulate a more specific question about which additional workloads would
strengthen the comparison.
Those eighteen inputs were subsequently included in the separate timed
extension reported in \cref{sec:results-structural-extension}.

ChatGPT helped with translation, algorithm explanations and discussions of review
findings. Concrete examples were useful when I was trying to understand the
relationship between sibling groups and the red--black finger tree. Codex
assisted with implementation changes, tests, validation tooling and manuscript
revisions. I found literature searches, architecture discussions and help
expressing technical ideas in English particularly useful.

For Appendix~\ref{app:backend-argument}, Codex helped formulate my extensions of
the arguments of Fung et~al. in English and LaTeX. Claude subsequently identified
an omitted premise and an incomplete diagnostic table. I decided to address both
findings and specified the required changes; Codex checked the premise against
the source paper and carried out the revisions. The omission showed me that
readable mathematical prose can still leave an argument incomplete. Successful
tests and fluent explanations therefore required different kinds of scrutiny.

I also questioned repeated qualifications that obscured the main findings and
requested a more direct presentation. For small edits whose intended wording was
already clear, working directly on the text might have been faster. When revising
the introduction, I supplied replacement wording to remove a repeated description
of the two backends. Explaining such a local change and checking another version
offered limited additional benefit.

I am satisfied that both backends produced correct output in the formal
measurements and that the thesis reports the tree's slower measured runtime
alongside its stronger structural bound. I am less satisfied with the incomplete
documentation of some earlier decisions and AI assistance. An independent human
review of the locality and mixed-operation arguments would also strengthen my
confidence in the work. In future projects, I would record decisions and their
reasons as they arise. Responsibility for understanding and defending the
submitted work remains mine.
